What happens on the call

  1. Minutes 0–5

    What is actually broken

    You describe the problem. We listen and ask about who feels it and what it costs each month it continues.

  2. Minutes 5–12

    Source system reality check

    What data exists, how it is structured, and what state the source systems are in. This is where estimates move most, so we do it early.

  3. Minutes 12–17

    Budget and sequencing

    Whether your budget and our estimate are in the same range, how much your team can absorb, and what order the work should happen in.

  4. Minutes 17–20

    A direct answer on fit

    Whether we can help, what we would propose, and when you would get a written proposal. Or an honest no, with a suggestion of who is better placed.

What we will ask you to bring. Nothing prepared. If you have an architecture diagram or a recent cloud bill it speeds things up, but the call works fine without either.

Before you write

What information helps in a first email?
One paragraph on the problem, roughly what your stack is, and any timing constraint. That is enough to know whether a call is worth either of our time. Nothing formal is needed.
Will you sign an NDA before we talk?
Yes, standard mutual NDAs usually within a day. Heavyweight vendor-onboarding programmes with portals, insurance schedules and multi-week procurement cycles usually mean we are not the right size of supplier, and we will say so early rather than consume a quarter of your time.
Do you charge for the scoping call?
No. The twenty-minute call is free. Deeper discovery on a large programme — where we examine systems and produce a written scoping document — is a paid engagement, and we will tell you before that line is crossed.
How do payments work internationally?
Invoices in USD, EUR, GBP or AED, paid by international wire, or by Visa or Mastercard through our secure card payment page. Typically fifty per cent on signing for a first engagement, reducing for repeat clients with a payment history.
Are there clients you will not take?
We decline equity-only arrangements, hourly generalist work below our engagement minimum, and any engagement where scope is genuinely undefined at the point of contracting. We also step back from procurements where a declarable conflict exists — see our conflict-of-interest position.
